# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: group and family trip organizers who need one shared, editable itinerary instead of a group chat full of screenshots. They centralize bookings and share a clean schedule with the people travelling with them.

Other audiences from the product docs (backpackers and multi-city travelers; budget-conscious travelers) are secondary and are not the design priority for now.

## Product Purpose

TripPlanner lets travelers plan, organize, and manage international itineraries in one place. It holds attractions, flights, accommodation stays, and free-form time blocks on a single per-day calendar, with a rough multi-currency budget estimate. Success means a group can agree on one itinerary without juggling emails and screenshots.

## Positioning

The differentiator is logistics on one shared calendar: attractions, flights, and stays are scheduled together and shared with collaborators who edit the same itinerary. Competitors such as Wanderlog and TripIt focus on raw logistics data and can feel like spreadsheets. Mood-based discovery (Hidden Gems, Instagrammable, Vibrant Nightlife) is a supporting feature, not the core claim.

## Operating Context

- Trips are planned on desktop and phone in a web browser. There is no native app.
- Groups plan together: one owner invites collaborators by email, and edits are last-write-wins.
- Bookings are made elsewhere. TripPlanner records them; it does not book anything.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Accounts with JWT sessions, login, and password reset.
- Trips can be private or public. Public trips are browsable through Explore.
- Shared attraction database searchable by country, city, or category, with mood tags.
- Per-day calendar supporting attractions, flights, and free-form slots, including overnight blocks.
- Stays (residences) reusable across trips with different dates.
- Budget and currency tracking per trip.
- Undecided: none recorded.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: TripPlanner. No logo or palette is fixed; no visual identity is binding yet.

## Evidence on Hand

No testimonials, case studies, customer counts, or press are on hand. Future work must not invent them.

## Product Principles

1. One shared source of truth for a trip's schedule, so groups stop juggling confirmations.
2. The calendar is the core surface; discovery and budget support it.
3. Record bookings people already made. Do not promise booking or payment.
4. Keep logistics legible: dense information must stay scannable on phone and desktop.
